# v2.1.2 — 2026-10-25

Bugfix release — closes a money-duplication exploit in the matching engine, fixes several net-flow accounting holes that pushed prices in the wrong direction, and makes the built-in test suites stop crashing and stop leaving data behind in your world.

## Bug Fixes

| Tag | Change |
|---:|---|
| ![bugfix] | <details><summary>Buy-then-sell loop could multiply money without limit</summary><br>A large limit order placed at the prevailing market price filled **entirely at its own limit price** before the price reacted; the price only moved afterwards. Selling back into that move and repeating compounded a balance without bound.<br><br>**Root cause:** a market's net player item flow — the figure the price model uses to track real player supply and demand — was only credited when an order *finished*. A large order therefore filled completely at the pre-impact price, and the equilibrium only learned about the purchase once it was too late to charge for it. Separately, `TargetPriceBot` sized its orders from total orderbook depth including resting player orders, so a player's own order inflated how much the bot was willing to trade against them.<br><br>**Fix:** net flow is now credited per fill rather than on completion, so the price responds while a large order is still filling. The bot sizes from virtual depth only. Reported and diagnosed by [@johnhkchen](https://github.com/johnhkchen) (#36).</details> |
| ![bugfix] | <details><summary>Market orders could skip a better-priced resting order</summary><br>`processBuy` and `processSell` walked a copy of the resting-order priority queue. A `PriorityQueue` copy is in *heap* order, not priority order, so the best available price was not always matched first — a market sell could fill at 99 and 95 while skipping a resting bid at 97, ending the market at the wrong price.<br><br>**Fix:** the snapshot is now sorted with the queue's own comparator before matching, so the best price is always taken first. Reported by [@johnhkchen](https://github.com/johnhkchen) (#36).</details> |
| ![bugfix] | <details><summary>Server crash when running the order-request test suites</summary><br>`ActiveOrdersRequestTestSuite` and `CreateOrderRequestTestSuite` constructed bank users through a three-argument `User(UUID, String, boolean)` constructor that BankSystem no longer provides, so both suites died with a `NoSuchMethodError` the moment they ran.<br><br>**Fix:** all 18 call sites now use the two-argument `User(UUID, String)` form. Semantics are unchanged — the dropped flag defaulted to `false` at every call site anyway.</details> |
| ![bugfix] | <details><summary>Test runs hijacked and mutated live player markets</summary><br>The suites asked for markets by item and relied on get-or-create semantics, which silently adopted the world's real `GOLD_INGOT` and `IRON_INGOT` markets and then traded against them. They also claimed fixed bank account numbers (#2 and #3) that a real player could already own, and wrote price history rows onto live market IDs.<br><br>**Fix:** every suite now runs against synthetic scratch markets, scratch accounts and negative scratch market IDs that cannot collide with anything in the world.</details> |
| ![bugfix] | <details><summary>Item-for-item trades did not move either market's net player flow</summary><br>Inter-market orders were excluded from net-flow tracking by a guard whose comment claimed the volume was "tracked at inter-market level" — no such tracking existed anywhere. Worse, when a leg filled against a resting player limit order the *counterparty* was still credited while the leg itself was not, so the market recorded net player buying when the true net was zero — moving the price model in the **wrong direction**.<br><br>**Fix:** both legs of an item-for-item trade now credit their own market — the item given away registers as supply, the item received as demand, exactly as if the player had sold one and bought the other. Covers both the market-order and limit-order inter-market paths, including the leftover "dust" sweep.</details> |
| ![bugfix] | <details><summary>Reserved funds leaked when an inter-market walk dropped a resting order</summary><br>`consumeBuySideDepthAtPrice` / `consumeSellSideDepthAtPrice` filled real player limit orders and moved real bank balances, then removed the order directly from the orderbook without firing the consumed/cancelled callbacks. Three things were skipped: the net-flow credit, the order-history record, and — when the remainder was dropped because no bank existed for the traded item — `unlockRemainingFunds`, so the player's reservation stayed locked forever.<br><br>**Fix:** both paths now route dropped orders through the market so the normal bookkeeping runs. A related defect was fixed alongside: the pending-removal list could hold the same order twice, firing its callback twice; it is now notified exactly once, with cancel taking precedence.</details> |
| ![bugfix] | <details><summary>Allowed-items list showed an unresolvable "air" row after a market-merge test</summary><br>`MarketMergeConsolidationTestSuite` restored its ItemID registry snapshot before the bank item-filter snapshot; in that order a filter entry outlived the registry row it pointed at and rendered as `air` in the allowed-items screen. The same suite also used `disallowItemID` to undo an allow — but that call *blacklists* the item rather than un-allowing it, so a second run of the suite failed with `Cannot create market for blacklisted item`.<br><br>**Fix:** the snapshot now carries the bank's `ItemFilterSnapshot` too, and restore runs filters-first, registry-second. The blacklisting undo was replaced with a true removal.</details> |

## API

| Tag | Change |
|---:|---|
| ![feature] | <details><summary>`IPluginOrderBook.getRealVirtualVolume(double, double)` — virtual-only depth over a price range</summary><br>Returns the orderbook depth between two prices counting **only** virtual liquidity, excluding resting limit orders. Use it instead of `getRealVolume(double, double)` when sizing orders that are meant to move the price: resting orders at a single price level absorb volume without moving it, so counting them overstates how much trading is needed.<br><br>Declared as a `default` returning `0` ("no virtual depth information available"), so existing plugins keep compiling unchanged. Implementations backed by a real orderbook should override it.</details> |

## Internal

| Tag | Change |
|---:|---|
| ![hardening] | <details><summary>New shared `TestFixture` — in-game test runs leave the world untouched</summary><br>All ten server-side test suites now allocate their resources through a single `TestFixture`. It mints synthetic scratch ItemIDs behind a marker key, hands out scratch users, bank accounts and markets, and exposes one `cleanup()` that drains them in dependency order: plugin caches → markets → accounts → users → item filters → ItemID registry.<br><br>A full run previously leaked roughly 30 bank users, 20 bank accounts, permanent rows in the ItemID registry, orphaned price-history rows and plugin cache entries. It now leaks none of them. `cleanup()` is idempotent and never throws, so a failing test still tears its own state down.<br><br>The registry and item-filter snapshot is taken in `setBackend()` rather than in the constructor, so the fixture can never capture an empty registry.<br><br>**Note:** only resources created by this and later runs are tracked. Registry rows minted by *earlier* test runs under the old random markers are not re-requested and therefore are not cleaned up retroactively.</details> |
| ![improvement] | Market-merge consolidation tests use a deterministic marker instead of a random UUID, so their scratch state is recognisable across runs |
